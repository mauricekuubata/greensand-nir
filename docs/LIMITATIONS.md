# Limitations

*Plain-language summary: what this project cannot tell you. Read before citing.*

1. **Computed, not measured.** Every library spectrum is calculated from pure-material spectra with a physical mixing model. Real sand has grain coatings, oolitic clay, burnt clay, fines and dead clay that the model does not include.
2. **No coal in the USGS library.** Seacoal is represented by kerogen (BK-Cornell), the organic matter coal forms from. It matches seacoal's darkness but not necessarily its exact absorption bands near 1700 and 2300 nm. With carbon black instead, the PLSR seacoal detection limit (dry, lab noise) is 0.3% instead of 0.5%.
3. **Red iron oxide stand-in.** The bright synthetic hematite in the USGS library (GDS27) cannot reproduce the measured AUC increase at any particle size (it is brighter than the sand from about 1135 nm onward). The darker fine natural hematite GDS69 (<10 um fraction) is used instead. Foundry red iron oxide products differ; this is the most important stand-in to replace with a measured spectrum.
4. **Effective sizes are fitted, not measured.** Seacoal (37.7 um) and iron oxide (2.48 um) sizes were fitted to 24 dry laboratory AUC values from one study, one sand, one bentonite. They are chosen from a 45-point logarithmic grid (1-300 um), so neighbouring candidate sizes differ by about 14%; the reported sizes are not more precise than that. Leaving out one DC2 group at a time moves them within 33.12-48.86 um and 1.3-3.66 um. The iron oxide value is far below the wavelength, where Hapke theory is approximate: treat it as an empirical parameter.
5. **Shared-baseline offsets in the lab data.** The measured AUC values contain a component shared by all additive levels at each DC2 level (DC2 3%: 50.1, DC2 6%: 171.5, DC2 9%: 158.8, DC2 12%: 277.8 AUC units). The model cannot produce this from composition, so the calibration treats it as a per-group offset. It most likely comes from the single 0% additive sample per DC2 level (packing, measurement session, mirror reference). Without the offsets, computed and measured AUC differ by 187.6 units RMSE (with offsets 37.0). Consequence: the library does not reproduce the measured increase of additive AUC with DC2 content.
6. **Bentonite type.** The paper calls DC2 "Southern Bentonite" in the methods and "Western Bentonite" in figure legends. The library uses sodium montmorillonite SWy-1 (western). With calcium montmorillonite SAz-1, the PLSR seacoal limit is 0.3%.
7. **Water model is uncalibrated.** Moisture is a uniform film with pure geometric path length (path factor 1.0). It ignores darkening from refractive-index matching, water held between clay layers, and compaction. Real moist green sand is likely darker with stronger water bands. Raising the path factor to 4 changes the moist-sand PLSR limits to 0.5% seacoal and 0.15% iron oxide. Use the moisture extension as a first approximation until measured moist spectra exist.
8. **Instrument and noise are modelled.** Noise tiers are stated signal-to-noise levels plus random packing gain, offset and tilt. They are not measured from your instrument's replicate scans. The instrument response width (15 nm) is assumed. A 25 nm width gives a seacoal limit of 0.5%.
9. **Reference standard.** The lab used a mirror reference in an integrating sphere; the library is relative to a perfect white diffuse reflector. Differences of this kind cancel in relative AUC and in calibrations built within one instrument, but absolute absorbance values are not comparable.
10. **Detection limits depend on the rule.** Limits use a 5% false-alarm rate and 95% detection on the stated level grid; a different rule or a finer grid gives different numbers. Benchmark test labels are public, so the leaderboard runs on trust.
11. **Scope.** Dry library and one moisture model; no compaction, temperature, or sand-system ageing effects; additives other than seacoal and red iron oxide (cereal, wood flour, other carbon sources) are not included.
